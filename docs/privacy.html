AshlarOS — Privacy

AshlarOS is a Linux distribution. It runs on your machine, and the parts of it that touch a network do so between your machine and whoever you told them to talk to. There is no AshlarOS account, no telemetry in the installed system, and no server of ours in the middle of anything you connect to.

Accounts you connect

Settings → Calendar and Settings → Mail configure software on your computer (vdirsyncer, khal, aerc) to talk to a provider you choose — your own server, or Fastmail, iCloud, Google and so on.

  • Credentials stay on your machine. Passwords go into your login keyring, or into a file readable only by you. We never receive them.
  • Calendar and mail data stay on your machine. They are synced directly between your computer and your provider. Nothing is copied to us, and there is nothing of ours in the path to copy it with.
  • Where OAuth is used, the sign-in happens in your browser with the provider. The resulting token is written to a file on your computer and used from there. We do not see it, hold it, or refresh it on your behalf.
  • Nothing is shared with third parties, because nothing is collected to share. Your data is not sold, transferred, or used to train anything.

Your provider has its own privacy policy, and it governs what happens to the data once it reaches them. That relationship is between you and them; we are not a party to it.

What this website does record

Two things, both on iso.ashlaros.download and packages.ashlaros.download:

  • A count of completed image downloads — the filename of the image and the number one. No IP address, no browser, no identifier of any kind is recorded with it, so the counts cannot be attributed to anyone.
  • Leaderboard entries for the game on the download page — the name you type into it, your score, and the day. Type anything you like; there is no account behind it and nothing links it to you. Pick a pseudonym if you would rather.

The site is served by Cloudflare, which processes requests on our behalf and keeps its own operational logs. We do not add analytics, advertising, or tracking of any kind on top of that — there are no cookies and no third-party scripts on these pages.

The installed system

A fresh installation phones nothing home. It checks for package updates against our repository and the Arch and CachyOS mirrors, which is an ordinary package manager talking to a file server, and it does so because you or your update timer asked it to.

Everything else that leaves the machine is something you configured: a calendar account, a mail account, a weather location, a model provider for the Ask key. Each is off until you turn it on, and each talks to the service you named.

Changes and contact
